## What the profile showed

On clean `05d57c5` one keep-alive GET, no sockets, was about 1,165 ns and still returned the 131-byte keep-alive response. The pieces that remained were `proto_admit` about 460 ns (the offer cache was about 87 ns; five `muro__sess_conn_step` calls and the 1 KB result fill were the rest), `host_copy_response` about 260 ns, and `host_in_policy` plus `host_in_bind` about 170 ns. The syscall path was already one `read`, one `write`, and one `epoll_wait` per roughly 32 requests. It was left as it was.

After the two commits the same microbench was 819 ns. `host_copy_response` was 78 ns, 131 bytes, 0 arena allocations. `httpRender` on the same inputs was 190 ns and 17 allocations. `host_in_policy` was 75 ns and `host_in_bind` was 80 ns. Four direct `muro__sess_conn_step` calls were still 190 ns. The admit path skips that fill on a hit.

A throwaway `rdtsc` build of the live 4-worker server (not committed; its three runs were 241,150 / 243,453 / 242,037) counted about 26 ready events per `epoll_wait`. One worker's totals, at a 2.904 GHz constant TSC:

| region | per call | per request |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `muro_http_step` | 1,625 ns, two calls | 3,251 ns |
| `read` | 1,751 ns elapsed | 1,751 ns |
| `write` | 10,653 ns elapsed | 10,653 ns |

`read` and `write` include time the process was off CPU. The 131-byte body is one `write`. The deadline scan walks 1,024 slots of about 3 KB and costs about a microsecond per wait, which is 47 ns once the batch is 26. That did not become a commit.

The in-server step is about 3.3 µs against an 819 ns microbench of the same two calls. The connection state lives in a slot of about 3 KB (`HttpStep` 1000 bytes, `HostIn` 2256 bytes), and 128 of those are live. That gap is cache traffic across connections, not one more book function.

## Fixes

Each number is 4 workers, keepalive, GET, c=128, median of three 5-second runs after a discarded warmup, 0 errors, 0 non-2xx. The 4-worker cell moves about 2% from one sitting to the next under `powersave`. A change was kept only when the three new runs sat above the three old ones.

### `6b56a0d` Write a keep-alive response in one buffer

`httpRender` concatenated the status line, the date, the connection header, and the length, copying at each join. `render_direct` writes those pieces once into the caller buffer. `httpConn` still chooses keep-alive versus close. `httpCode` still chooses the status digits, including `rdShow` for a code the book does not spell. The date bytes are the slice the caller passed. A code or connection slice the direct writer cannot use falls back to `httpRender`. The finished response is not stored.

Runs before: 219,695 / 221,504 / 221,702. Runs after: 226,952 / 228,241 / 229,385. All three after runs sit above all three before runs. p50 on the median run was 533 µs, p99 1,142 µs.

### `ed20046` Remember a pure session move instead of refilling it

`muro__sess_conn_step` returns a kilobyte result. `step_val` keeps the status and the next state. Eight slots, round-robin, keyed by the session word (`key`, `at`, `stuck`, `nat[0]`, `set[0]`) and the value (`dir`, tag, bit, nat, `n`, constructor pointer). A hit does not call the stepper. A different pick bit or a different constructor pointer misses, so keep versus close still comes from the book. Nested values (`a`, `b`, `names`) are not remembered. `proto_refuses` still calls the stepper. The expected string is display text computed from the state.

The response is not cached. Close mode still takes `httpConn(1)`.

## Stop

The keep-alive cell moved twice, then the next measured pieces were smaller than the sitting noise. `host_in_policy` and `host_in_bind` are 75 ns and 80 ns in the hot microbench. `arm_soonest` is 47 ns per request and `find_slot` is 57 ns. A further memo of `headersDone`, `expectCode`, `methodView`, and `versionView` might save on the order of 100 ns. The three-run bar on this cell is about 2% (the same binary sat at 242,880 and at 236,145). A 100 ns memo would not clear that bar cleanly, and it would not by itself pass h2o.

What is left is the gap between the 819 ns socket-free step and the 3.3 µs the same step takes inside the server, plus the `write` of 131 bytes, whose elapsed time was about 11 µs on these shared hyperthreads under `powersave`. Packing the hot fields out of the 3 KB connection slot is the change most likely to touch the in-server step. I did not do it: the profile does not isolate one field, and a layout change big enough to matter is also big enough to get wrong. Close mode is a separate accept path and was left alone.

I would try the header-view memo only on a fresh profile that still shows those calls above the noise, and I would not expect it to close the remaining h2o gap on its own.
