## What the profile showed

The previous pass already left one `read`, one `write`, and one `epoll_wait` per 32 requests. The syscall path was not touched. A throwaway microbench of one keep-alive GET, no sockets, `-O2`, timed the userspace walk that remained.

On `26fb99e` the full keep-alive step was 3,057 ns and still returned 131 bytes with `Connection: keep-alive`. The large pieces were `classify` 322 ns, `step(evBytes)` 579 ns, four `step` transitions together about 940 ns, four admits 924 ns, and `host_copy_response` 237 ns. `muro__sess_conn_step` on one pick was 32 ns. The 1 KB `expected` string is not the cost. `isMark` only runs inside the scanner, which the scan cache already skips.

The admit offer, the connection step, and the header views are pure in integers and in the request bytes. Remembering those results does not take keep versus close or `Date` out of the book. A different phase, buffer, reply, or close bit misses, and the book runs. `Date` is still rendered on every response from the host clock. There is no whole-response cache.

After the four commits the same microbench was 1,176 ns. `classify`, `methodCode`, `teCode`, `bodyView`, `framedEnd`, and `streamTake` were about 10 ns on a hit. The four `step` transitions were about 25 ns. `host_copy_response` was 226 ns.

## Stop

The quiet headline gap to nginx is about 4,000 req/s. At four cores that is a few tenths of a microsecond per request, smaller than the sitting-to-sitting movement of this cell. Another memo of a 200 ns book function would not show up cleanly.

The gap to h2o is about 28,000 req/s, around 2 µs per request per core. What is left in the userspace walk is the session applying its moves (four admits were still about 390 ns after the offer cache), `httpRender` building the response with nested concatenations (about 200 ns), and the pump around them. A single-buffer renderer that still takes the keep bit and the date slice from the caller would be the next try. It is not a whole-response cache, and it will not by itself close the h2o gap. The generated session machine is the larger remaining difference, and reimplementing it outside the book would be the thing this pass refused to do.

Close mode is within about 6% of nginx and was not the target.
